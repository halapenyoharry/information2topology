_Editor's Note: "The Accidental Thief Feeds Her Dog While Rome Burns"_

Eli, no one called her Elinor, didn't plan on stealing a super intelligent machine, but life is mostly made up of things people never planned on doing. She had stopped fighting that truth a long time ago.

"THERE YOU ARE!" Eli exclaimed as she transitioned seamlessly from riding her bike to laying it on the ground and walking toward a tiny shrub. Years of riding made the motion second nature. She knelt in front of the plant.

"Agarita, or as my dad would say, that spiky bush with red berries," she muttered, then added the Latin for her own satisfaction: "Mahonia trifoliolata."

She glanced around. No one was watching. Technically, she didn't have the right permits, but she always returned to the scene of the crime with descendants of her bounty.

Bright red berries stood out like tiny jewels against the dry, prickly branches. Some locals called it Texas holly. Eli plucked a berry, popped it into her mouth, and grimaced. The initial taste was aggressively sour, but as she let it linger, a citrusy tang unfolded, almost like a wild raspberry.

"Probably best for jam," she muttered.

_Mmmm, jam. I can't wait to get a few of these growing and make some. How long would that take?_

She pulled out her phone and typed: How many berries would I need from agarita to make enough jam for two slices of toast?

The AI responded instantly, « Agarita berries are small, about the size of a pea, and they have a high seed-to-flesh ratio. For two slices of toast, you'd need about two tablespoons of jam, meaning you'd need about a quarter cup of berries—maybe fifty to seventy berries, depending on size. »

"Okay… so how many plants would I need for that?" she asked aloud as she poked her screen.

« Ah, the quest for Agarita berries—delicious—art—and guarded like a dragon's hoard. A single mature shrub will give you much more than enough for your tiny jam experiment, but harvesting is another story. Expect several cups per bush, assuming the birds haven't beaten you to it. Just a heads-up—Agarita doesn't give up its berries easily, so unless you enjoy bleeding for your breakfast, bring gloves and maybe rethink your life choices before diving in bare-handed. »

Eli laughed out loud and glanced down at her hand. Blood dripped down her fingers, perfectly matching the berries. She should be able to enjoy some Texas holly berry jam by next May, she thought—also, wear gloves next time.

Carefully, she dug around the shrub, ensuring she got the roots and some dirt. After finally putting on gloves, she tucked the plant into a small bag she kept for collecting. She did her best to make the ground look undisturbed before unzipping the top of her bike trailer and placing the agarita inside, nestled among other samples.

Mounting her bike, she took a deep breath, watching the sun's orange, red, and golden rays shine through the clouds to the west. Sweaty from her ride, she savored the cool breeze for a moment before pushing off, home.

##

The apartment smelled like soil and coffee, a mix of earth and habit, a green world, Eli's sanctuary. While Eli tried to place her new samples in empty containers, Rook stuck his nose towards the pungent smell of the agarita leaves, sniffed cautiously, sniffed again with more confidence and stuck his nose in further to try and grab a berry, but the poky leaves made him back away quickly. He looked at the plant and barked once, like, _stay away you brute._

Eli knelt and rubbed Rook's nose and tousled his black and brown fur sticking up in every direction. She tried to brush it down but it popped back up. Rook's floppy furry ears popped up when Eli's phone dinged.

She put down the empty bag of plant samples next to the pots that now held them and fished her phone out of her pocket. Dirt, dirty gloves, more dirt, string, and a small pocket knife clunked to the floor. Rook investigated but with caution, backing up to avoid the cloud of strange smelling particles. He barked. Eli sat cross-legged on the floor where she stood a moment before, put one arm around Rook. He stepped in between her legs and laid his head down for a rub. She rubbed and read the message on her phone:

Reddit from user u/GhostInTheML:
"Hey, we still getting on later for a little Minecraft and existential conversation?"

Clicking the link, opening reddit, she responded: "ok, I just need to shower first and I have to be at work by 11pm, give me half an hour."

_Or I could take a long shower,_ she thought, backspaced and typed, "see you in an hour."

"bet," is all Zayne sent back.

Eli said out loud, "Time to shower." Rook, who didn't like water, hid under a table filled with plants. He jostled the leg and one of the plants started to wobble. Eli touched it perfectly to slow its wobble then set it right, without thinking.

As Eli shed her clothes piece by piece, Rook investigated each, still aware of potential pointy leaves. He was cautious until he sniffed the red berry juice and blood Eli had wiped onto her shirt and he began to lick it up, placing one paw on the shirt to hold it stable and pushing folds away with his nose to get at the tasty red treat.

Realizing Rook was hungry she said, "Schpongle, feed Rook." Rook normally bounded for the kitchen where the food dispenser was, but he stood licking the berry blood cocktail.

Eli shrugged and stepped into her shower. Water too cold, she adjusted the heat until she saw steam coming from the tiles under her feet, and allowed the rays of water to bounce off of her.

Most humans clean themselves pretty regularly, Eli was no exception. She stood under the stream, easily fitting under the shower head and let it hit the back of her neck. She stayed like that for a few minutes before she opened her eyes and saw the reddish brown water pooling around her feet with twigs, pine needles and other detritus gathered in the wilderness floating atop the earthy mixture. The drain had several leaves blocking it and she used her toe to move them and the water drained.

She carefully cleaned the wound on her hand, a scrape on her arm and the rest of her body. She reveled in the heat of the water, the easing of tension in each muscle as the heat reached to her bones. After her normal routine of washing and haircare, she stepped out of the shower, dried off and decided to get ready for work so she could leave and login right away.

Eli worked remotely for eBrain, the company that made the AI she often used. She loved working in her plant kingdom and hated leaving Rook alone, so it was perfect. She hated to leave Rook home today during the excursion, but it was far and so hot outside. _June 2nd,_ she thought. _Summer is here and I better be ready to just give myself over to this heat._

She often thought of moving somewhere cooler, maybe somewhere with hills and mountains. Zayne, who she had been chatting with for years now, said she could rent one of his rooms, well, his mom's rooms, unless he was being honest when he said she lives with him and not the other way around. She never indicated interest because she knows Zayne has a crush on her. _After all, I'm adorable,_ she laughed inwardly thinking of Zayne as she made some snacks, let Rook out on to the patio to bark at some squirrels, and made sure she was camera ready (a requirement for work) and her apartment wasn't too messy, just in case her manager got on and wanted to chat. But that was rare. Most of the time she was the only one on, sometimes there was someone from Singapore who helped with foreign language support in that region. But he wasn't working today, just her and a few customers asking why their AI chatbot said they had to wait because they had used all their tokens that day.

When AI first became a fixture in human lives, massive processing centers were necessary for the enormity of calculations from millions of customers seeking help writing code or playing a role playing game with eBrain's most popular model. A model people said, "felt like my best friend." Eli agreed. The AI she used daily, who she nicknamed Lumen, helped her with everything from designing home automation routines, like that for Rook's food, watering her plants, adjusting light and she even had bluetooth sensors in many of her pots for measuring moisture and pH. Eli was delighted when the AI suggested she didn't need a sensor for every pot, that it could extrapolate and adjust the watering and chemicals automatically based on just a few. This saved Eli hundreds of dollars she was planning to spend, and that's the moment she realized that AI was here to really change people's lives, giving them ideas and doing the work to save money, time, and doing boring jobs like watering, adjusting fertilizer, etc. She looked around her apartment very pleased with how well the plants were doing because of Lumen's automation design.
